**CAREER: Perceivability: Enabling Safe and Secure Autonomy via Synergistic Control, Observation and Learning** ([NSF Award #1942907](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award?AWD_ID=1942907))

Autonomous systems such as drones and self-driving cars must learn about their surroundings while staying safe, even when sensors are imperfect and communication cannot be fully trusted. This project introduces **perceivability**, a structural property describing whether a system can safely build knowledge about its environment over time, given its dynamics, sensing, communication, learning method, and time limits. If an environment is not perceivable, the project asks which system parameters could change to make it so, and how control and observation can work together to gather knowledge more safely.

The research is organized around four tasks:

- **Foundations of perceivability:** *clarity* as a measure of information gain, and reachability-based characterizations of perceivable sets under constrained system dynamics.
- **Enhancing perceivability through synergistic control and observation:** estimation and control over finite time horizons, including control barrier functions that respect input constraints and state-estimation errors, and the *gatekeeper* framework for online safety verification of nonlinear systems.
- **Experimental demonstration and case studies:** multi-agent dynamic coverage of spatio-temporal environments, certifiably-correct mapping for safe navigation despite odometry drift, and flight experiments with a custom-built quadrotor.
- **Education and outreach:** course development (Multi-Robot Systems, ROB 416/516), open-source hardware and software ([github.com/dasc-lab](https://github.com/dasc-lab)), and K-12 summer activities.
