The [Center for Autonomous Air Mobility and Sensing (CAAMS)](https://www.caams.center/) is an NSF Industry-University Cooperative Research Center that brings together universities, industry, and government to address the core technical challenges of autonomous air mobility, from assured autonomy and AI for aviation to unmanned aircraft systems and sensing. The University of Michigan joined CAAMS in 2023, with Prof. Dimitra Panagou serving as the UM site director.

Our work in CAAMS, **Tactics in SWARMS for Trusted Foundational Autonomy** (System-Wide Adversarially-Robust Multi-Agent Systems), develops methods for safety-critical control under different classes of uncertainty:

- **Nominal mission planning:** multi-objective planning of probabilistically safe trajectories in stochastic environments with reactive obstacles.
- **Safety verification (gatekeeper):** distributionally robust safe planning and control for systems with hard-to-model, non-Gaussian, nonlinear stochasticity, which runs in real time on high-dimensional systems such as an F-16 fighter jet and a racecar.
- **Estimation, adaptation, and uncertainty quantification:** safe online adaptation and conformal-prediction-based uncertainty quantification for learning-enabled controllers, integrated with control barrier functions and MPC.
- **Multi-agent safety:** decentralized planning and safety filters that remain safe despite noisy dynamics, restricted communication, and impaired localization, with applications to urban air mobility, search and rescue, and contested environments.
